This page runs the demo. The exchange is a simulator with a clock of its own: you can play a trading day whenever it suits the audience, at a speed of your choosing, while simulated customers trade in the background. Everything the system does follows the exchange's clock, not the wall clock.

Exchange clock

The exchange's clock is the system's business time. Its trading day (09:30-16:00 New York), its close and the end of its business day (16:30) drive everything after them: orders are taken only while the market is open, day orders expire at the close, and the accounts close their books when the business day ends. The clock only goes forward; going back means resetting the demo (reset-stack.sh).

Background traffic

Simulated customers using the same commands a real customer's clicks send: market and limit buys, sells of what they hold, limit orders left resting and some cancelled, deposits and withdrawals. They keep the system busy so the customer UI and the views below have something to show. The rate is per exchange minute, so it follows the clock's speed. Orders go in only while the market is open, with a burst after the open; outside the session the customers only move money. They have accounts of their own (BG-...), so Alice sees only hers.

What happens during a trading day

  1. Before the open (until 09:30). The exchange takes no orders: the customer UI's order ticket says the market is closed. Customers can still deposit and withdraw.
  2. The open (09:30). The market opens and orders flow in, with a rush in the first minutes - the background traffic's burst. Watch the orders count climb below, and Alice's Trade page take orders again.
  3. Continuous trading. Each order travels placed - submitted - filled - booked: the exchange fills it, the fill comes back as an event, and the account books the trade and releases what it held.
  4. The close (16:00). The exchange stops taking orders. Day orders still open expire.
  5. The end of the business day (16:30). The exchange closes its business day and says so with an event. The system then
    • rolls each account's books into the next business day as it is next touched (a new generation in the Essentials admin console's closing books),
    • settles the day's trades on the next business day (T+1),
    • clears deposits that are due,
    • receives the clearinghouse's and the bank's statements, and reconciles them; anything that does not match becomes a reconciliation case at the next close.
